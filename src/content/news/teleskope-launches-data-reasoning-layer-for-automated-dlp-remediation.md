**Teleskope** launched its **Data Reasoning Layer**, a proprietary architecture inside its data-security platform that connects classification, policy-driven decision-making, and native remediation in one loop.

The company's argument is straightforward: many DSPM tools find sensitive data, and many DLP tools enforce at a movement point, but teams still get stuck deciding what a finding means and how to fix it safely. Teleskope is trying to collapse that gap by classifying data in business context, choosing an action based on the customer's policy, and enforcing the action in the same platform.

### From Findings to Actions

The launch includes a new classification engine, AI Search, policy builder and ingestion, and automated remediation across **OpenAI**, **Slack**, and **Claude**. Teleskope says high-confidence cases can be resolved in under two seconds, with human review reserved for lower-confidence decisions.

That distinction matters for DLP and DSPM buyers because remediation backlogs are often where posture programs lose momentum. Finding externally shared sensitive data, stale access, or AI-exposed content is useful only if the team can reliably decide whether to redact, revoke access, quarantine, notify, or delete.

### What This Means for DLP

Teleskope is not pitching another static policy catalog. The more interesting claim is a decision layer that understands business context before acting. Practitioners should validate how those decisions are explained, how reversible actions behave in production systems, and whether customer-defined policies translate cleanly into automated enforcement without creating a new false-positive queue.

The broader signal is familiar: data-security vendors are moving from "where is the data?" toward "what should happen to it right now?" That is where DSPM, DLP, AI governance, and workflow automation are starting to converge.
