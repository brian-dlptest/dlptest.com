**Geordie** raised a **$30 million Series A** led by **Balderton Capital**, with participation from **Crosspoint Capital**, **General Catalyst**, and **Ten Eleven Ventures**. SecurityWeek reports that the round brings Geordie's total funding to **$36.5 million**.

The London-based startup focuses on security and governance for enterprise AI agents. Its platform is described as giving organizations real-time understanding of each agent, what it can access, how it behaves, and what risk it introduces. Geordie also offers **Beam**, a runtime remediation suite intended to shape and constrain agent behavior while systems are in use.

### Why data-security teams should care

This is not classic DLP or DSPM. The relevance is the access-control layer forming around autonomous agents. If an AI agent can read documents, invoke tools, call SaaS APIs, or act under delegated identity, data security teams need more than an inventory of models. They need to know which agents can reach sensitive data, whether behavior has drifted, and what enforcement point can stop or narrow the action.

That overlaps with the same buyer questions showing up around DSPM, SSE, and insider-risk programs: who or what can touch sensitive data, what is the intended business context, and where can controls be applied before exposure occurs.

### DLPTest take

Geordie sits adjacent to DLP rather than inside it. The funding is still worth tracking because agent governance is becoming a control-plane problem for data protection teams. If these platforms mature, they may become a source of context that DLP and DSPM vendors either integrate with or try to absorb directly.
