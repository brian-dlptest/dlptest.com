**Ent** emerged from stealth with a **$100 million seed round** led by **Decibel**, with participation from **Sequoia**, **Crosspoint Capital Partners**, **Craft Ventures**, **Shield Capital**, **Felicis**, and **In-Q-Tel**. The company is positioning its platform as intent-aware workspace security: a prevention layer that evaluates what humans and AI agents are doing before risky actions become incidents.

The product runs as a lightweight endpoint agent, hosted in the customer's cloud, and observes behavior across applications, browsers, workflows, data movement, and local runtimes. Ent says it can apply customer-defined policy through just-in-time interventions rather than waiting for a completed event to become a detection, investigation, or incident-response workflow.

### Why This Belongs in the DLP Conversation

Ent is not describing itself as a classic DLP vendor, and buyers should not evaluate it as a regex-and-channel-coverage replacement. The relevant overlap is the control point: many data-loss events now look like ordinary work until context changes the interpretation. A developer moving source code, an employee using an AI tool, or an autonomous agent acting across systems may require intent and workflow context before policy can make a useful decision.

That is close to the same problem endpoint DLP and insider-risk teams have been trying to solve for years: how to distinguish legitimate business activity from exfiltration, misuse, or preventable mistakes without flooding analysts with low-confidence alerts.

### What To Validate

Practitioners should test how Ent explains intent, how policy maps to existing data classifications, and whether intervention actions are precise enough for high-friction workflows. The funding size and founding team pedigree make the launch notable, but the durable question for DLP buyers is whether intent-aware endpoint controls can reduce data-loss risk without becoming another noisy behavioral analytics queue.
