**Seclore** launched **ARMOR DSPM**, a new generally available capability inside its ARMOR platform for discovering, classifying, prioritizing, and remediating sensitive-data risk across enterprise and AI environments.

The announcement is not just another "AI governance" checklist item. Seclore is trying to connect data context to action: what the data is, why it matters to the business, who or what can access it, and what should happen before the data is consumed by copilots, agents, or other AI workflows. The company describes this as a "Semantic Triad" of content, context, and intent rather than relying only on regex or predefined classifiers.

### Why This Matters for DSPM Buyers

The DSPM market has matured past basic sensitive-data discovery. Buyers increasingly ask whether a platform can tell which exposures matter, route findings into existing security and privacy workflows, and trigger protection or monitoring without turning every scan result into a ticket.

ARMOR DSPM is explicitly aimed at that gap. Seclore says findings can feed the broader ARMOR platform for automated classification, protection, monitoring, and audit readiness, while the DSPM component runs in a dedicated cloud environment without passing sensitive data to third-party LLMs.

### What To Validate

For DLP and data-security teams, the key diligence questions are practical: which repositories are covered today, how "intent" is inferred, how remediation is approved or rolled back, and whether Seclore's data-centric controls can follow sensitive information after discovery. The stronger the handoff from DSPM insight to enforcement, the more relevant this becomes for programs trying to secure AI without standing up a separate governance-only tool.
