**Seclore** announced general availability of **ARMOR DSPM**, a new capability inside its ARMOR platform for discovering, understanding, and protecting sensitive data across enterprise and AI environments.

The launch is a clear DSPM move, but Seclore is framing it through a protection lens rather than a pure discovery lens. The company says ARMOR DSPM evaluates data using content, context, and intent, then prioritizes findings based on business risk so teams can decide what must be classified, protected, monitored, or remediated.

### Why This Matters for DLP Teams

DSPM projects often start with inventory: where sensitive data lives, who can access it, and which repositories are exposed. The harder operational question is what happens next. If a tool finds a risky source-code file, regulated record, or trade-secret document, the security team still needs a path from discovery to enforcement.

Seclore's angle is that DSPM should feed downstream controls, including classification, persistent protection, AI-DLP, monitoring, and audit evidence. That is relevant for DLP practitioners because AI adoption increases the importance of pre-access context. If copilots, agents, and AI workflows can reach sensitive content, teams need to know not just that the content exists, but whether the data is protected before a model or agent can use it.

### What To Validate

Buyers should test how quickly ARMOR DSPM produces useful classifications after repositories are connected, how well the content-context-intent model reduces false positives, and whether remediation workflows fit existing classification and DLP operations.

The architecture is also worth diligence. Seclore says the solution runs in a dedicated cloud environment, makes no external API calls, sends no data to third-party LLMs, and is initially deployable on AWS and Google Cloud. For regulated enterprises, those deployment and data-handling claims may matter as much as the discovery engine itself.
