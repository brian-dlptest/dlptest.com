**Seclore** announced general availability of **ARMOR DSPM**, a new capability inside its ARMOR platform for discovering, understanding, and protecting sensitive data across enterprise and AI environments.

The company is positioning ARMOR DSPM around fast onboarding, contextual classification, business-risk prioritization, and remediation. Seclore says the product begins discovery and classification once repositories are connected, then helps teams identify sensitive data before it is accessed by AI applications, copilots, agents, and enterprise AI workflows.

### The DSPM Angle

This is a product launch rather than a funding or M&A headline, but it fits the broader DSPM market shift: discovery alone is no longer enough. Buyers increasingly want classification, ownership context, access risk, and some path to remediation or protection in the same workflow.

Seclore's existing story is data-centric protection, so the interesting part is whether ARMOR DSPM can connect posture findings to controls that persist as information moves across users, applications, cloud platforms, and AI systems. That is where DSPM either becomes an operational data-security program or remains a scanner that creates another backlog.

### What To Validate

Security teams should examine which repositories are supported at launch, how findings are prioritized, and whether remediation actions integrate with existing DLP, rights-management, and data-governance processes. Seclore also emphasizes deployment in a dedicated cloud environment with no external API calls, no data passed to third-party LLMs, and no sensitive data retained. That architecture claim will matter for regulated buyers evaluating DSPM in countries with strict sovereignty and localization requirements.
