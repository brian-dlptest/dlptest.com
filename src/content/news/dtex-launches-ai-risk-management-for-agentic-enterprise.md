**DTEX** introduced an expanded **AI Risk Management** product aimed at enterprise use of generative AI tools, copilots, and autonomous AI agents. The release is in private preview, with broader availability expected next quarter.

The important DLP angle is that DTEX is not just positioning this as an AI inventory feature. The company says the product monitors prompts, responses, uploads, downloads, and AI-generated content, then correlates that activity with behavioral baselines and prompt lineage to distinguish normal experimentation from risky or malicious behavior.

### Why it matters for DLP buyers

This is another sign that insider-risk vendors are recasting DLP around intent, lineage, and agent behavior rather than static rule catalogs alone. DTEX already sits in the insider risk, user activity monitoring, UEBA, and DLP stack; adding AI-agent context gives buyers a way to evaluate whether the same telemetry can explain both human-driven and AI-driven data movement.

The questions for practitioners are practical: where does enforcement happen, how much endpoint visibility is required, and how quickly does a private-preview capability mature into controls that reduce exfiltration risk without creating another alert queue?
