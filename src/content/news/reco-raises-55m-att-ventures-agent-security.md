**Reco**, the Israeli-founded SaaS and AI agent security vendor, raised another $55 million, bringing total funding to $140 million. The round includes a strategic investment from AT&T Ventures, which reportedly started out as a Reco customer. A customer turning into an investor means a lot in a crowded market.

Reco maps the relationships between agents, human identities, applications, permissions, and data. Security teams can see which AI agents are active, what credentials and scopes they're using, and which systems and data they can reach. Then they can find and revoke access that's excessive or no longer needed. That's access governance and exposure mapping, closer to CIEM or identity governance than to content inspection DLP.

Reco claims coverage across more than 280 applications and AI services, including **OpenAI**, **Anthropic**, **Microsoft Copilot**, **Salesforce**, **ServiceNow**, and **Workday**. As enterprises connect LLM agents to core SaaS systems, one over-permissioned agent identity can do a lot of damage, and most DSPM and DLP tools weren't built to track agent identities.

By its own positioning, Reco doesn't replace those tools. The question is if its agent and identity graph fills a gap they leave open: knowing not just where sensitive data is, but which autonomous agents have standing permission to touch it.
