**Ent**, an "intent-aware workspace security" startup, emerged from stealth with **$100 million** in seed funding, reported as one of the largest seed rounds in cybersecurity history. **Decibel** led the round, with **Sequoia**, **Crosspoint Capital Partners**, **Craft Ventures**, **Shield Capital**, **Felicis**, and the CIA's venture arm **In-Q-Tel** participating.

The San Francisco company was founded by **Elias Manousos** (CEO) and **Brandon Dixon**, the team behind **RiskIQ**, acquired by Microsoft in 2021, and then **Microsoft Security Copilot**. They argue that AI shrinks the time between compromise and impact so much that prevention has to be the main goal again, not detection after the fact.

Ent is a lightweight endpoint agent that runs small AI models on the device, so there's no trip to the cloud. It watches activity across applications, browsers, AI workflows, data movement, and local runtimes. It evaluates the intent of both people and AI agents at the moment of action, applies your policy, and steps in before an incident happens. It's hosted in the customer's own cloud for data sovereignty and is generally available for Windows, macOS, Linux, and as a browser extension.

### Why this is a DLP story

Ent's use cases read like a DLP and insider risk brief: detect insider risk, govern AI usage, prevent data loss, stop last-mile threats, and investigate incidents with behavioral context. Think about someone giving remote control of their machine to a person outside the company, or pasting sensitive data into an unsanctioned AI tool. Endpoint, web, and SaaS DLP tend to catch those late or not at all.

Ent's argument is that EDR and SIEM collect at the process and file layer and respond after the action is done, while work now happens across the browser, apps, and AI assistants. That puts it closer to endpoint DLP and insider risk than to AI policy tools.

### What to watch

A $100 million seed is a huge number, and the founders plus In-Q-Tel and Sequoia show investors still like AI-native controls that live on the endpoint. If you're evaluating it, a system that steps in based on *inferred* intent has to be right often enough that it doesn't block legitimate work and teach people to ignore it. The sub-second on-device inference claims are Ent's own, with no independent benchmarks yet. Ent says it's already deployed across Global 2000 customers in hospitality, financial services, and defense, and it will show the platform at Black Hat USA 2026.
