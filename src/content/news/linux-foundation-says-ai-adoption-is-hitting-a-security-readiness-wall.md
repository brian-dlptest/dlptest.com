**The Linux Foundation** released its 2026 State of Tech Talent Report with a blunt finding for AI programs: security readiness has become the leading obstacle to adoption and innovation.

The report says security concerns rose from **17% in 2024 to 48% in 2026** as the top barrier to AI success. It also found that **57% of organizations report capacity gaps in AI security and risk management**, while 40% say they are understaffed in cybersecurity and compliance roles.

### Why This Belongs in Data Security

The report is talent-focused, but the operational problem maps directly to data governance. AI systems need access to enterprise data to deliver useful work, and the same teams being asked to accelerate AI are also being asked to prove that sensitive data, prompts, model outputs, and automated actions remain controlled.

### Upskilling Over Hiring

Linux Foundation Education argues that organizations are responding by upskilling existing staff rather than relying only on outside hiring. The report claims upskilling offers advantages in business context, team cohesion, retention, total cost, and quality of work.

### Market Implication

For DLP, DSPM, and AI governance vendors, the finding is a buyer-readiness signal. Products that assume customers already have mature AI security programs may run into adoption friction. Tools that simplify discovery, policy authoring, monitoring, and audit evidence are better aligned with the readiness gap the report describes.
