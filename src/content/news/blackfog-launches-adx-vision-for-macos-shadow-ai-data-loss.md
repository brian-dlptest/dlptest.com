**BlackFog** launched **ADX Vision for macOS**, extending its shadow AI detection, governance, and prevention platform to Apple endpoints. The company says the release lets enterprises apply a consistent AI data-loss policy across Windows and macOS devices to stop sensitive data from reaching unsanctioned LLMs.

The release is relevant because macOS endpoints are common among executives, engineers, designers, and other high-sensitivity users, but many AI governance controls still focus on browser extensions, network proxies, CASB paths, or SaaS-side integrations with sanctioned AI vendors.

### The Endpoint DLP Angle

BlackFog is making an architectural argument: AI-bound data should be inspected on the device before it is encrypted or transmitted, regardless of whether the request came from a browser, native desktop app, IDE integration, or local agent. That matters because shadow AI usage is not limited to a single approved chatbot domain.

For DLP teams, the question is not whether every AI prompt needs to be blocked. It is whether the control can see enough data movement to make policy enforcement meaningful. If a developer uses an IDE assistant, an executive uses a native desktop AI app, or a local agent sends context to an LLM, proxy-only and SaaS-only approaches may have blind spots.

### What To Validate

Buyers should test how ADX Vision identifies AI-bound flows on macOS, how policies map to existing classifications, and whether enforcement remains usable for mixed Windows and Mac fleets. The release also raises a practical deployment question: endpoint-native AI DLP may close important visibility gaps, but it still needs clear exception handling, investigation records, and user experience controls to avoid becoming another high-friction blocker.
