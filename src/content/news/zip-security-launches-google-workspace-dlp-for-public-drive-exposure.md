**Zip Security** launched a **Data Loss Prevention** capability for **Google Workspace** focused on publicly exposed Google Drive files. The company says the product connects directly to Workspace, scans up to 180 days of sharing history, and ranks exposed files and users using metadata such as file names, permissions, and sharing activity.

The launch is narrower than a full enterprise DLP suite, but the problem is familiar: collaboration platforms make it easy for sensitive files to drift into public or over-broad sharing states. Zip is aiming the control at lean IT and security teams that need visibility and remediation without building custom Drive audits or deploying a specialist DLP platform.

### What Makes It DLP-Relevant

Zip says the product analyzes metadata rather than file contents, then presents an AI-generated plain-language risk assessment for exposed files. That design choice is worth testing carefully. It may reduce privacy concerns and speed deployment, but metadata-only inspection will not catch every sensitive record hidden behind an innocuous file name.

The operational value is in the remediation path: admins can revoke public access directly from the Zip console instead of opening tickets or switching into a separate Google admin workflow. For smaller organizations, that kind of closed-loop action can matter more than another dashboard of findings.

### Practitioner Lens

This is a practical collaboration-DLP story, not a platform consolidation headline. Buyers should validate how Zip prioritizes files when names are vague, whether shared-drive and external-domain edge cases are covered, and how actions are audited. The larger signal is that DLP vendors are continuing to package focused controls around specific SaaS exposure paths where accidental disclosure is common.
