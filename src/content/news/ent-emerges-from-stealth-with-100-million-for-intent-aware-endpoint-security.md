**Ent** emerged from stealth with **$100 million** in seed financing led by **Decibel**, with participation from **Sequoia**, **Crosspoint Capital Partners**, **Craft Ventures**, **Shield Capital**, **Felicis**, and **In-Q-Tel**. The company is positioning itself as an intent-aware workspace security platform rather than a conventional endpoint detection tool.

The core idea is that the endpoint is where employees and AI agents actually perform work: moving between applications, copying data, uploading files, using browsers, invoking local runtimes, and interacting with AI assistants. Ent says its lightweight agent observes those workflows, evaluates human and agent intent at the moment of action, applies customer-defined policy, and intervenes before an incident is complete.

### Why DLP Teams Should Watch

Ent is not describing a classic content-signature DLP product. The relevant claim is that intent and behavioral context can support several adjacent controls from the same endpoint layer: **data loss prevention**, **insider risk management**, AI usage governance, last-mile threat prevention, and post-incident investigation.

That is a familiar promise in a new AI-era package. Legacy DLP programs often fail because policies are expensive to tune, alerts are noisy, and legitimate work can look risky when content inspection sees only a file, keyword, or destination. Ent is betting that richer endpoint telemetry and local AI reasoning can make the intervention decision earlier and with more context.

### What To Validate

Buyers should test whether Ent can explain interventions well enough for users and investigators, not just block actions. The product's value will depend on whether it can distinguish legitimate business workflows from risky exfiltration across browsers, local apps, AI tools, and agent-driven activity without creating a new alert queue under a different name.

The size of the seed round makes the market signal hard to ignore. Endpoint security, DLP, insider risk, and AI governance are starting to converge around the same practical question: can a control understand what a user or agent is trying to do before sensitive data leaves the environment?
