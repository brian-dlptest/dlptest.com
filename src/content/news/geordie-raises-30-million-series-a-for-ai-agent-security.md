SecurityWeek reports that **Geordie** raised **$30 million** in Series A funding led by **Balderton Capital**, with **Crosspoint Capital**, **General Catalyst**, and **Ten Eleven Ventures** participating. That brings the London startup's disclosed funding to **$36.5 million**.

Geordie is building a security and governance platform for autonomous AI agents. It's about more than filtering prompts. It's about knowing which agents exist, what systems and data they can reach, how they behave at runtime, and what risk they add as enterprises move from pilots to production.

### Why this is a DLP story

For DLP teams, AI agents are a new kind of privileged user. If a person copies a file to an unsanctioned destination, endpoint, web, or SaaS DLP can see it. An agent calling tools, reading knowledge sources, and passing context between systems can create the same exposure without looking like classic exfiltration.

Geordie gives you a real-time view of agent access and behavior, plus **Beam**, a runtime remediation suite that constrains what agents do. That puts it closer to data access governance, AI security posture, and insider risk than to AI policy tools.

### Market context

This follows recent deals around AI data access graphs and endpoint DLP, including Zscaler/Symmetry Systems and Cyera/Genie Security. Trade coverage doesn't agree on Geordie's valuation, so stick to the funding size and the investors. Security investors will still back independent products for agentic AI if they can show who or what is touching enterprise data.
