**Zscaler** announced a set of agentic AI security updates for its **Zero Trust Exchange**, moving beyond the May Symmetry Systems acquisition headline into concrete controls for how agents connect, access data, and run on devices.

The launch includes **Zscaler AI Broker** for securing MCP and agent-to-agent communications, **Zscaler Endpoint AI Security** for finding AI-related risks in browsers, plugins, extensions, and local AI tools, and the **Zscaler AI Access Graph** for mapping relationships among agents, identities, applications, models, and data sources.

### From Visibility to Enforcement

For DLP and DSPM buyers, the interesting part is not the "agentic AI" label by itself. It is the combination of data lineage, identity context, and endpoint reach. Zscaler says the access graph, powered by Symmetry technology, can help teams see which agents and identities communicate with which models, applications, and data sources, then apply policy through the Zero Trust Exchange.

That is a familiar problem in data security posture management: knowing where sensitive data lives is not enough if an AI agent can reach it through inherited permissions, browser plugins, or MCP-connected tools.

### What Practitioners Should Test

The practical diligence question is whether Zscaler can make these controls operational without forcing teams to stitch together separate AI inventory, DLP, endpoint, and identity views. Buyers should test whether prompt extraction, endpoint AI visibility, and data-source lineage produce enforceable policy decisions rather than another dashboard of AI assets.

This release also shows why the Symmetry acquisition mattered strategically: access graphs are becoming a control-plane primitive for agent governance, not just a reporting feature adjacent to DSPM.
