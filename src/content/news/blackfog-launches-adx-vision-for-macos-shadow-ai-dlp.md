**BlackFog** launched **ADX Vision for macOS**, extending its endpoint-native shadow AI detection and prevention platform to Apple devices. The company says enterprises can now apply a consistent AI data-loss policy across Windows and macOS endpoints to stop sensitive data from leaving through unsanctioned LLMs.

The product is built around BlackFog's anti data exfiltration architecture. Instead of relying on browser plugins, network proxies, certificate interception, or SaaS-side integrations with specific AI vendors, ADX Vision for macOS runs as a native macOS system extension that can inspect AI-bound data flows on the device before they are encrypted or transmitted.

### Why macOS Coverage Matters

For many organizations, Mac endpoints are disproportionately used by executives, engineers, designers, and other high-value users who handle source code, strategy documents, customer data, and intellectual property. Those same users are often early adopters of desktop AI clients, IDE assistants, local agents, and unsanctioned browser tools.

That creates a practical DLP gap. Browser-based controls may catch prompts sent through a web chatbot, and CASB or SSE controls may see managed cloud traffic. They are less reliable for native desktop apps, developer workflows, local models, or AI agents that move data outside the clean proxy path.

### Practitioner Lens

BlackFog's release is not a broad DSPM announcement. It is a channel-coverage story for endpoint DLP and insider-risk teams. The question buyers should test is whether endpoint-native AI flow inspection can identify sensitive content and risky destinations without drowning teams in noise or blocking legitimate work.

The announcement also reinforces a broader pattern: AI data-loss controls are moving closer to the endpoint because not every AI exfiltration path is visible from the network. For mixed Windows and macOS estates, consistent enforcement across both operating systems is becoming part of the DLP evaluation checklist.
