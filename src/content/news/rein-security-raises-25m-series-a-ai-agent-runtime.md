**Rein Security**, a New York and Tel Aviv startup, raised a $25 million Series A co-led by **Glilot Capital** and **Sienna Venture Capital**, bringing total funding to $35 million. SecurityWeek reported it first, with PRNewswire and Calcalist backing it up.

There are a lot of "secure the AI agent" startups now. Rein's claim to fame is runtime. It uses patented sidecar technology that sits next to agents as they run, so security teams can see every line of code an agent runs and every resource it touches, and don't have to rely only on pre-deployment scanning or policy review. For data security teams, Rein says it enforces guardrails "dynamically and in real time with business-aware controls based on user, role, and data sensitivity." That puts it with **Archestra** and **Hush Security**, which are also building controls for what agents can read, write, or exfiltrate.

Calcalist reports revenue has grown eightfold since January, with the platform securing thousands of agents running millions of actions. Named customers reportedly include **Lemonade** and **Dun & Bradstreet**, which suggests real production use and not just pilots.

"Control what the agent can touch" is turning into its own category, somewhere between DLP policy enforcement and identity and access governance. What Rein is building also looks like a natural extension of data classification and policy engines, so DSPM and DLP vendors may end up as partners or competitors here.
