**DTEX** introduced **AI Risk Management**, extending its behavioral-intelligence platform to monitor how employees, copilots, and autonomous AI agents interact with enterprise data.

The release fits the insider-risk and DLP conversation because DTEX is not only inventorying AI tools. It says the product monitors prompts, responses, uploads, downloads, AI-generated content, and data movement to detect leakage of source code, intellectual property, and other sensitive information.

### Intent Becomes the Control Problem

Classic insider-risk tools were built around human behavior: unusual access, abnormal downloads, off-hours activity, or policy violations near a departure date. Agentic AI complicates that model because an autonomous workflow may touch sensitive data while operating inside approved permissions and an apparently legitimate task.

DTEX is positioning AI Risk Management around that gap. The company says it can distinguish human versus AI-driven actions, track "Computer Use" AI activity, and correlate prompt lineage, behavioral baselines, and agent actions over time. In one early deployment described in the announcement, DTEX says it surfaced an autonomous agent exposing sensitive data despite the agent operating within its intended workflow and permissions.

### Practitioner Lens

For DLP teams, the important question is whether AI-agent telemetry can become enforceable risk context rather than another dashboard. Buyers should test how DTEX classifies shadow AI, how it maps prompts and data movement back to users and identities, and whether agent-driven exfiltration signals can trigger timely response without over-flagging normal automation.

The product is currently in private preview, with broader availability expected next quarter. That makes proof-of-control testing especially important: the category is moving quickly, but the operational bar is still whether security teams can prove what data an agent touched, why it touched it, and what stopped the exposure.
