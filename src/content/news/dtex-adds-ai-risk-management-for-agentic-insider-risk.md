**DTEX Systems** introduced an expanded **AI Risk Management** product aimed at the messy overlap between insider risk, GenAI adoption, and autonomous agents operating with access to enterprise data.

SiliconANGLE reports that the product is designed to discover sanctioned and unsanctioned AI usage across users, endpoints, browsers, applications, and embedded AI workflows. More importantly for DLP teams, DTEX says it monitors prompts, responses, and data movement so analysts can spot leakage of source code, intellectual property, and other sensitive information before it turns into a formal incident.

### Why This Fits Insider Risk

Traditional insider-risk tooling usually starts with human behavior: unusual downloads, odd application use, risky file movement, or a departing employee moving data to personal storage. DTEX is extending that model to agents by trying to separate human-driven actions from AI-driven ones and by preserving more lineage around what an agent was instructed to do.

That distinction matters because a legitimate AI workflow can still expose regulated data or IP while staying inside nominal permissions. If the signal is just "an authorized identity accessed data," the risky context may arrive too late.

### What DLP Buyers Should Watch

The product is in private preview, with broader availability expected next quarter. Practitioners evaluating this category should press on the evidence trail: how prompts are captured, how agent actions are attributed back to a human or service identity, and whether remediation can be enforced without turning every GenAI interaction into another alert queue.

DTEX's move also reinforces a broader market pattern: DLP, user activity monitoring, UEBA, and insider-risk controls are converging around AI data movement rather than staying in separate tooling lanes.
