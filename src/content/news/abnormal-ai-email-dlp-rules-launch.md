**Abnormal AI** announced a three-part expansion of its email security platform on August 27: a new Control Center for policy management, native **Email DLP Rules**, and upgrades to its AI Phishing Coach training. The interesting part is the direction. Abnormal is taking the behavioral engine it built to catch inbound phishing and business email compromise and pointing it at outbound data loss.

That's different from how email DLP has usually worked. **Proofpoint**, **Mimecast**, and **Microsoft Purview** built their outbound controls on keyword matching, regex patterns, and static classification rules. Those work against known patterns, but they're known for false positives and a motivated insider can get around them. Abnormal's pitch is that the same anomaly detection that spots a spoofed vendor invoice can flag outbound messages that don't match how a sender normally communicates. In Abnormal's words, modern attacks "look like normal business activity until the behavior gives them away."

So can a model tuned to catch messages written by attackers also catch insiders, like a departing employee quietly forwarding client lists or a contractor sending sensitive attachments to the wrong person? And can it do that without the tuning work that comes with rule-based systems? That's the question to test if you're evaluating this.

It's also not clear if this is an add-on SKU for existing customers or a way into bigger data security deals. If you already run Abnormal for inbound protection, native DLP rules make it easier to put email security and DLP under one vendor. If you're buying for DLP, ask if behavioral detection is mature enough for compliance-driven outbound controls, or if it works better on top of your existing policy engine.
