**Ent** emerged from stealth with **$100 million** in seed financing led by **Decibel**, with participation from **Sequoia**, **Crosspoint Capital Partners**, **Craft Ventures**, **Shield Capital**, **Felicis**, and **In-Q-Tel**. The company is founded by RiskIQ veterans **Elias Manousos** and **Brandon Dixon**, who later worked on Microsoft Security Copilot.

Ent's pitch is not another EDR alert queue. The company describes an "intent-aware Workspace Security" layer that runs AI reasoning on endpoints and browser-extension deployments, watching activity across applications, browsers, workflows, data movement, and local runtimes before a risky action completes.

### Why DLP teams should care

The most relevant part for DLP and insider-risk buyers is the timing of control. Ent says Global 2000 customers are already using the platform to detect insider risk, govern AI usage, prevent data loss, and investigate incidents with behavioral context. That puts the product near the boundary between endpoint DLP, insider risk management, AI governance, and classic endpoint protection.

If the architecture works as advertised, it addresses a familiar gap: many data-loss events look like normal work until the moment a user or AI agent moves sensitive information into the wrong place. Ent is betting that local, real-time intent analysis can intervene earlier than systems that reconstruct incidents after the fact.

### Procurement lens

A $100 million seed round sets a high bar for execution, but the investor mix and founder history make the launch notable. DLP buyers should still test the hard parts during evaluation: how policies are expressed, what user friction looks like, whether file/content inspection is deep enough for regulated data, and how cleanly the tool complements existing EDR, SIEM, SOAR, IAM, and DLP controls.
