**Glow** emerged from stealth on July 22, 2026 with $180 million in an all-equity Series A at a $1.2 billion valuation. Sequoia Capital, Cyberstarts, Greenoaks, and Redpoint Ventures led the round, with Index Ventures, Lux Capital, and others participating.

The hook for data security teams is shadow AI. Glow cites its own data showing that regular AI tool usage on corporate devices, authorized or not, jumped from 15% to 45% in a year. That's a real problem. Unmanaged GenAI on endpoints is where a lot of sensitive data is quietly leaking today.

But look at how it works. Glow does prevention and application control, not content inspection. It controls everything that runs on the endpoint, using AI agents to map the environment, score risk in real time, and enforce policy automatically, deciding which software is allowed and which gets removed. That's allowlisting and software governance, closer to app control than to endpoint DLP.

**So where does this fit next to your DLP program?** It's next to it, not overlapping. Software control can shrink shadow AI by keeping unsanctioned tools off devices, but it doesn't inspect what data goes into the AI tools you *do* allow. The question is if Glow adds data-aware controls later. Today the pitch is about what runs, not what leaves.

The team is worth noting. CEO Roi Tiger spent nine years at Meta, most recently as VP of Engineering. CTO Omer Singer was Head of Cybersecurity Strategy at Snowflake, which is a data security background. VP of R&D Ophir Arie came from the same role at Claroty.

Two caveats. Glow hit unicorn status with no publicly available product, and its early metrics are self-reported with no independent benchmarks. (The valuation also varies across coverage, but the company and most trade press say $1.2B.)

Keep an eye on it as endpoint security, and ask about the data control roadmap once a product ships.
