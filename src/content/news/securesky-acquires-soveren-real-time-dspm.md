**SecureSky**, a provider of Exposure Management and AI-based Managed Detection and Response (MDR) services, acquired **Soveren**, a Data Security Posture Management (DSPM) startup that goes beyond the at-rest scanning most of this category does.

Soveren's platform combines at-rest DSPM with real-time, eBPF-based network analysis, so you can see sensitive data both at rest and in motion. The in-motion part is what's interesting. It doesn't just do periodic scans of storage buckets and databases. It inspects and classifies encrypted TLS traffic at scale, reportedly with zero impact on production systems, and it goes after the false positives that have been a problem for regex and pattern classifiers for years. It also tracks usage anomalies and changes in third-party data flows across cloud and on-prem, which is closer to DDR (data detection and response) than classic DSPM.

Customers include **Booking Holdings**' **Agoda** and **OpenTable**. Soveren raised a modest $10 million from a well-connected group of investors, including founders and CEOs from **Airbnb**, **Slack**, **Datadog**, **Palo Alto Networks**, **MuleSoft**, and **Deel**, along with Sir Richard Branson's family office and European VC **firstminute capital**. That's small next to DSPM heavyweights like **Cyera** or **Cyberhaven**, but those investors suggest people in the industry liked the technology.

Vendors without their own sensitive data classification are buying it, not partnering for it or building it, like **Cyera** did with **Trail** and **Proofpoint** did with **Normalyze**. At-rest-only DSPM is becoming table stakes, and eBPF-based runtime and network detection looks like where vendors will compete next.
