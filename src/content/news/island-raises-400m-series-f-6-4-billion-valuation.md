**Island**, the enterprise browser vendor, closed a $400 million Series F that values the company at $6.4 billion, more than double where it stood in 2024. **Evolution Equity Partners** led the round, joined by existing backers **Prysm Capital**, **Sequoia Capital**, **Coatue Management**, **Cyberstarts**, and **Insight Partners**.

The interesting part isn't just the size of the check. It's how Island describes itself: "Island created the first Enterprise Browser to protect the primary place people work. From there, we expanded into data protection and DLP, then built a next-generation SASE architecture from the endpoint outward." So Island is telling the market the browser is now a real place to enforce DLP, next to endpoint agents and network or CASB inspection.

Enterprise browsers have mostly been sold for access and isolation (stop malware, control SaaS access), not as a full DLP replacement. This is one of the largest raises in the browser security category so far, and it shows investors believe the browser can take real DLP and SASE budget. That's especially true as controls extend to agentic AI workflows, where the browser is often the first place sensitive data leaves a sanctioned boundary.

Expect this to put pressure on other enterprise browser and SSE vendors to build in DLP-grade classification and enforcement, and not partner for it. You can already see that in Akamai's earlier acquisition of LayerX. If you're a DSPM or endpoint DLP vendor, keep an eye on Island's plan to compete "from the endpoint outward" over the next 12–18 months.
