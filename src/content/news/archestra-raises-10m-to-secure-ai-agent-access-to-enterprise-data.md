**Archestra** raised a **$10 million seed round** led by **20VC**, bringing total funding to $13.5 million for its open-source platform that brokers access between AI agents and enterprise data.

The company says multiple Fortune 500 customers are running Archestra in production, using it as a middle layer between agents and internal systems. Instead of letting an agent connect directly to tools such as Jira, Confluence, GitHub, Notion, SharePoint, Google Drive, or Salesforce, Archestra checks identity and policy, brokers access, and records the result.

### The Data-Control Gap for Agents

This is adjacent to DLP because the risk is not only whether an employee uploads a file into a chatbot. The emerging problem is whether autonomous agents can retrieve sensitive data, combine it with untrusted context, and then send it somewhere the organization did not intend.

Archestra's pitch is that deterministic guardrails, governance, and observability should sit close to the tool call and data-access layer. That makes it different from a generic AI governance dashboard and more relevant to security teams trying to understand how AI systems touch regulated or confidential data.

### What to Watch

For buyers, the proof will be in policy depth and deployment friction. A useful control plane for agentic workflows needs to understand identity, data sensitivity, tool permissions, audit requirements, and downstream exfiltration paths without forcing every AI team into a bespoke integration project.

The funding is another signal that AI-agent security is converging with classic DLP concerns: who or what touched sensitive data, why it happened, whether it was allowed, and whether the data could leave through an unexpected path.
