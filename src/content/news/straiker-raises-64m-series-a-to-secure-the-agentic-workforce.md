Mountain View-based **Straiker**, founded in 2025, raised a $64M Series A led by Marathon Management Partners, with Citi Ventures, Illuminate Financial, Workday Ventures, and returning backer Bain Capital Ventures participating. That brings total funding to roughly $85M.

The platform discovers AI agents across an environment, maps their access and behavior, runs adversarial testing before deployment, and then blocks threats at runtime. On its face, that's AI runtime and threat security and not data classification.

So why cover it here? Because the risk from agents is a data access problem. As agents get connected to CRMs, data lakes, file stores, and ticketing systems, the question goes from "is this file classified" to "what non-human identity can reach this data, and what will it do with it." Most DSPM and DLP deployments were built to watch users and endpoints, not agents acting for them. Agent access also often goes through service credentials and API scopes that classic DLP never sees.

Straiker sits next to DLP, DSPM, and insider risk, not inside them. But data exposure through non-human identities is becoming its own gap, and this category keeps drawing money.
