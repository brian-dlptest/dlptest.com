**Seclore** announced general availability of **ARMOR DSPM**, a new capability in its ARMOR platform for discovering, classifying, prioritizing, and protecting sensitive data across enterprise and AI environments.

The company frames the product around a "Semantic Triad" of content, context, and intent. In practice, that means Seclore is positioning ARMOR DSPM against discovery-only tools: not just finding regulated records or intellectual property, but trying to determine why a data set matters, who owns it, and which remediation path should follow.

### DSPM Moves Toward Action

The useful signal for practitioners is the emphasis on risk prioritization and downstream enforcement. DSPM programs often stall when discovery creates a backlog of findings that security teams cannot confidently rank or resolve. Seclore says ARMOR DSPM can feed findings into security, privacy, and compliance workflows or extend them into broader ARMOR classification, protection, monitoring, and audit controls.

That posture fits the direction of the market: data discovery is becoming table stakes, while buyers increasingly ask whether a platform can help decide what to fix first and whether it can enforce protection without months of tuning.

### What To Validate

Security teams should test ARMOR DSPM against business-specific data such as source code, trade secrets, AI training sets, and board-level documents - not only common PII patterns. They should also review how the dedicated cloud deployment model handles data residency requirements, what evidence is produced for auditors, and how well remediation integrates with existing DLP or data-centric protection workflows.

For DLP and DSPM buyers, Seclore's launch is another sign that AI-readiness messaging is converging with concrete data-control requirements: know what sensitive data exists, understand whether AI or human users should touch it, and move from visibility to protection quickly enough to reduce exposure.
