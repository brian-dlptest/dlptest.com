**Seclore** launched **ARMOR DSPM**, a new capability in its ARMOR platform for discovering, classifying, prioritizing, and protecting sensitive data across enterprise and AI environments. The release frames DSPM as a prerequisite for safe AI adoption: before copilots, agents, or internal AI workflows consume data, security teams need to know what the data is, who owns it, who can reach it, and whether exposure risk is acceptable.

### Moving DSPM Beyond Inventory

Seclore's pitch is that discovery alone does not solve the buyer problem. A scanner can find source code, trade secrets, regulated records, or intellectual property, but security teams still need context to decide whether the finding is urgent and what remediation should follow.

ARMOR DSPM is built around what Seclore calls a **Semantic Triad** of content, context, and intent. In practical terms, that means the product is trying to distinguish business-critical exposure from pattern-matching noise, then route the result into security, privacy, compliance, or broader ARMOR protection workflows.

### What DLP and DSPM Teams Should Test

For practitioners, the meaningful question is whether Seclore can turn sensitive-data intelligence into action without creating another discovery backlog. Buyers should validate how quickly repositories can be connected, how well ownership and business context are inferred, and whether remediation integrates with the workflows teams already use.

The AI angle is also concrete enough to matter. Seclore says ARMOR DSPM helps identify and classify sensitive data before it is accessed by AI applications, copilots, agents, and enterprise AI workflows. That puts the launch in the same buying conversation as DSPM, AI data governance, and DLP enforcement: data teams want visibility, but they also need a path from finding to control.
