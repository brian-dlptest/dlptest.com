**Zip Security** launched a **Data Loss Prevention** capability for **Google Workspace**, focused on identifying and remediating publicly exposed Google Drive files. The company is positioning the feature for growing businesses that may not have a dedicated DLP team or a standalone enterprise DLP deployment.

The product connects to Google Workspace, analyzes file metadata, permissions, and up to 180 days of sharing history, then surfaces high-risk files and users. Zip says teams can revoke public access from the same interface, turning the workflow into a direct exposure-reduction loop rather than a manual audit followed by tickets.

### A Narrower, Practical DLP Wedge

This is not classic content-inspection DLP across every endpoint, browser, email, and SaaS channel. Zip's announcement says the product analyzes metadata, permissions, and sharing history rather than reading or storing file contents. That narrower scope is still relevant because public Drive exposure remains a common accidental data-loss path, especially in organizations that rely heavily on shared documents.

For buyers, the attraction is simplicity: find externally exposed files, understand why they are risky, and close access without stitching together Drive API scripts, manual spreadsheets, and separate remediation queues.

### What To Validate

Security teams should test whether Zip's metadata-driven approach catches the exposures they care about: old public links, sensitive project folders, customer files shared outside expected domains, and users whose sharing patterns create disproportionate risk. They should also confirm how exceptions are handled, whether audit evidence is preserved, and how the DLP signal connects to Zip's existing endpoint, identity, browser, and compliance controls.

The launch is a useful reminder that DLP is not only about detecting regulated strings in outbound traffic. In SaaS collaboration environments, excessive sharing permissions can become the leak, and lightweight remediation can matter as much as deep classification.
