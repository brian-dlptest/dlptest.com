**KnowBe4** announced two additions to its **Prevent** outbound email security product: a DLP rule builder and AI-powered misdirected content analysis. The company is positioning the update for small and mid-market organizations that need to stop accidental data loss without buying a heavyweight enterprise DLP program or relying heavily on professional services.

### What Changed

The DLP rule builder lets administrators create blocking rules for outbound email that contain sensitive data such as PII or financial records. KnowBe4's example is a practical one: block only when a message contains multiple sensitive data elements, reducing the false-positive burden that often makes email DLP hard to keep enabled.

Misdirected content analysis adds a recipient-aware check before a message is sent. Instead of only inspecting the payload, Prevent looks at whether the data appears to be headed to the wrong person and warns the user in context. That makes the control closer to coaching plus prevention than a pure quarantine workflow.

### Why DLP Buyers Should Care

Email remains one of the most durable channels for accidental data leakage, especially where DLP ownership sits with a lean IT or security team. The interesting part of KnowBe4's announcement is not that outbound email DLP exists; it is the attempt to package policy authoring and recipient analysis in a way that a smaller organization can operate directly.

For DLP practitioners, this is another signal that vendors are trying to reduce policy friction rather than simply adding more content detectors. The useful test will be whether Prevent can keep blocking logic understandable while still handling messy real-world cases: mixed internal and external recipients, forwarded threads, attachments with partial matches, and business-approved exceptions that should not train users to ignore warnings.

### Market Context

KnowBe4 is best known for security awareness and human-risk reduction, so the product framing leans into mistakes rather than malicious insider behavior. That can be a good fit for outbound email, where the common incident is an employee sending the right data to the wrong person. It also means Prevent sits adjacent to, rather than replacing, broader endpoint, SaaS, and web DLP controls.

The new features are expected to be available in June 2026.
