**Seclore** announced general availability of **ARMOR DSPM**, a new capability inside its ARMOR platform for discovering, understanding, and protecting sensitive data before it is used by AI applications, copilots, and agents.

The launch extends Seclore's data-centric security positioning into DSPM. Instead of treating discovery as a static inventory exercise, Seclore says ARMOR DSPM evaluates data through content, context, and intent so teams can prioritize findings by business risk and route them into protection, monitoring, or remediation workflows.

### Why this matters for DLP teams

The practical DLP angle is that Seclore is trying to make posture findings actionable. Security teams do not need more lists of exposed repositories if those findings cannot inform classification, access controls, rights management, or enforcement policy. ARMOR DSPM is aimed at the upstream side of that problem: deciding which sensitive data is safe for AI workflows and which needs tighter controls first.

The company also emphasizes deployment boundaries: dedicated cloud environments, no external API calls, no sensitive data retained, and no data passed to third-party LLMs. That will matter in regulated environments where AI data-security tooling has to pass the same data-residency and trust reviews as the systems it protects.
